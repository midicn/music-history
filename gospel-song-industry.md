1875 年，慕迪与桑基把廉价歌本卖进布道会场，宗教歌曲从此也是一门出版生意。

## 时间与地点

1873 至 1875 年，英国与美国。慕迪布道，桑基领唱，先在英国工业城市，再回到芝加哥与费城。会场多租用大厅或帐篷，座位按场次清空，歌本在入口处出售，观众可以带回家。歌本一册接一册地出，封面印着两位布道家的名字，后来干脆以《福音诗歌》为系列名。

## 发生了什么

桑基坐在小风琴前领唱，声音不大，句子清楚，歌词讲个人得救的故事。布道会通常先唱几首再讲道，歌曲把陌生听众聚成一群。歌曲多为分节歌，每节之后接一个重复的副歌，会众听两遍就能跟上。歌本把歌词、旋律与四部和声印在一起，价格低，印量大，还附索引、主题分类与编号，领唱报号，会众翻到就唱。出版商把布道会用过的歌编成系列，一次比一次厚，主日学校与祈祷会也拿去用，歌本还出口到英国与其殖民地。会场里另有一支志愿合唱队，坐在台后，负责在独唱之后把副歌接住。

## 改变了什么

宗教歌曲变成可销售、可复制的商品，也成为最大规模的集体歌唱形式。副歌结构后来进入乡村音乐、灵魂乐与流行歌，成为“抓耳”的模板。歌本取代了宗派 hymnal 在布道会上的位置，出版商与布道家形成共生关系。领唱者成为明星，风琴让音乐可以搬到任何帐篷里。布道会因此可以复制到任何城市，歌本成了标准化的节目单。

## 听这个转折

先听《九十九只羊》，独唱叙述，风琴只给和声骨架，结尾落在副歌的重复上。再听《他必再来》，副歌一句句抬高，会众容易加入。最后听《耶稣爱我》，旋律像儿歌，重复到不需要谱。转折前，教堂歌曲由唱诗班与管风琴承担；转折后，任何人买一本歌本就能在帐篷里唱。注意伴奏的克制，它不抢独唱，也不要求听众多懂和声。

In 1875 Moody and Sankey sold cheap songbooks into their preaching meetings, and sacred song became a publishing business.

## Time and place

Britain and the United States, 1873 to 1875. Moody preached and Sankey led the singing, first in industrial British cities and then in Chicago and Philadelphia. Meetings rented halls or tents, seats were cleared between services, and songbooks were sold at the entrance so that listeners could take them home. The books appeared one after another with the two evangelists' names on the cover, and the series soon took the title Gospel Hymns.

## What happened

Sankey sat at a small organ and led the singing, his voice modest and his words clear, the texts telling stories of personal salvation. A meeting usually opened with several songs before the sermon, and the singing gathered strangers into a crowd. Most songs were strophic, each verse followed by a repeated refrain that a congregation could join after two hearings. The books printed words, melody and four-part harmony together at low cost and in large runs, with indexes, subject headings and numbers the leader could call out. Publishers gathered the songs used at the meetings into series that grew thicker each year, and Sunday schools and prayer meetings adopted them. A volunteer choir sat behind the platform and caught the refrain after each solo.

## What changed

Sacred song became a commodity that could be sold and copied, and also the largest form of collective singing. The refrain structure later entered country music, soul and pop as a template for catching the ear. The songbook displaced the denominational hymnal at the meetings, and publishers and evangelists grew interdependent. The song leader became a star, and the organ made the music portable to any tent. A campaign could therefore be reproduced in any city, with the songbook as its standard programme.

## Listen for the turn

Begin with The Ninety and Nine, a solo narrative with the organ supplying only a harmonic frame and the ending resting on a repeated refrain. Then He Is Coming, its refrain rising phrase by phrase so a congregation can join. Finally Jesus Loves Even Me, a tune close to a children's song, repeated until no score is needed. Before the turn church song belonged to choir and organ; afterwards anyone with a book could sing in a tent.
