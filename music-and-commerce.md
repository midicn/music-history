## 核心问题

音乐要卖钱，也要卖东西。乐谱、唱片、广告歌和背景音乐把听觉变成交易。商业究竟腐蚀音乐，还是为它提供传播的燃料？

## 前后经过

19世纪乐谱出版商靠通俗钢琴曲打开家庭市场，拉格泰姆把谱子卖到全国。20世纪唱片公司与广播广告互相喂养，广告歌把品牌写进旋律。百货商店与电梯里的背景音乐用轻音乐调节购物节奏，1960年代摇滚又被广告业收编。21世纪流媒体按播放付费，短视频把十五秒副歌变成商品。

## 几个例子

乔普林《枫叶拉格》靠乐谱与钢琴卷成为畅销品；《演艺人》在钢琴卷与后来的电影配乐中反复变现；《铃儿响叮当》从感恩节歌曲变成零售季节的声音标志。三例分别对应出版、复制与品牌。

## 听这几处

## The core question

Music has to earn money, and music has to sell other things. Scores, records, jingles and background music turn listening into trade. Does commerce corrupt music, or does it supply the fuel that carries it?

## Before and after

Nineteenth-century sheet publishers opened the home market with popular piano pieces, and ragtime sold scores across the country. Record companies and radio advertising fed each other in the twentieth century, while jingles wrote brands into melodies. Department stores and lifts used light music to pace shopping, and rock was absorbed by advertising in the 1960s. Streaming now pays by play, and short video turns a fifteen-second chorus into goods.

## A few examples

Joplin's Maple Leaf Rag became a bestseller through sheet music and piano rolls. The Entertainer was monetised again and again, on rolls and later in film. Jingle Bells moved from a Thanksgiving song to a soundmark of the retail season. Publishing, copying and branding, one case each.
