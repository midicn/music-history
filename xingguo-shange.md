## 年代与地方

兴国在江西南部，贡水支流潋江穿过县境，四周是丘陵与山地。唱的是赣南客家话，与广东梅州、福建龙岩的客家话同属一个大区，声调与用词略有差别。兴国山歌在田间、山路、圩场唱，也在苏区时期的群众大会上唱。县城与圩镇是传播节点，山歌从山村传到镇上，再传回山村。

## 它是怎么来的

兴国山歌本来用于劳动与男女对答，一人起腔，对方接句，歌词即兴。20世纪30年代，兴国是中央苏区的模范县，山歌被用来扩红、支前、宣传，旧调填新词，唱得比从前更响。1949年以后，文化部门到兴国采录，山歌被整理成舞台节目，也进入音乐教材。改革开放后，老歌手陆续去世，录音与非遗保护成了主要保存方式。

## 声音的骨架

兴国山歌最醒目的是一声“哎呀嘞”，高音长呼，像是先向对面山头发个信号，然后才进入正句。正句多为四句，七字上下，叙事为主，句尾拖长并加装饰。旋律以五声为骨，羽调式常见，音域宽，跳进多。节奏自由，歌者按字调与呼吸伸缩。对唱时两人共用一套腔，靠词锋与反应分高下。

## 听什么

先听客家的开腔，注意第一声怎样从低处翻上去，再落回说话的音区。兴国山歌的“哎呀嘞”就是这个动作的固定化。接着听句尾的拖腔，那是歌者换气与思考下一句的时间。再听对唱里的接句，接得快的人往往只重复对方的句尾，再翻出新意。

## 常被误会的地方

常见误解是把兴国山歌都当成红色歌。苏区时期确实产生了大量新词，但曲调与唱法来自更早的山歌传统。也有人以为“哎呀嘞”只是一句随口喊的衬词，其实它是起句的信号，决定整首歌的高度与气口。兴国山歌与赣南采茶戏的茶歌也不是一回事，后者已进入戏曲。

## When and where

Xingguo lies in southern Jiangxi, where the Lian River, a tributary of the Gong, cuts through hills and uplands. The songs are in southern Jiangxi Hakka, closely related to the Hakka of Meizhou in Guangdong and Longyan in Fujian, with differences of tone and vocabulary. They are sung in fields, on hill paths and at markets, and in the 1930s also at mass meetings of the Soviet areas. The county town and the market towns were the nodes through which songs travelled from village to town and back.

## How it came about

Xingguo songs began as work songs and exchanges between young men and women, one voice starting and the other answering, words made up on the spot. In the 1930s Xingguo was a model county of the Central Soviet, and songs were used for recruitment, supply campaigns and propaganda: old tunes took new words and were sung louder than before. After 1949 cultural workers collected them, arranged them for the stage and put them into teaching materials. In recent decades, as the old singers died, recordings and heritage protection became the main means of keeping them.

## The sound's frame

The most striking feature is the cry "ai ya lei", a long high call that signals across the valley before the verse begins. The verse usually runs to four seven-character lines, mostly narrative, with long ornamented endings. The melody rests on a pentatonic frame, often yu mode, with a wide range and many leaps. Rhythm is free, stretched by speech tone and breath. In dialogue the two singers share one tune and compete through wit and speed of reply.

## What to listen for

Begin with a Hakka opening and hear how the first sound lifts from low to high and then settles back into speech register. The Xingguo cry fixes that gesture. Then listen to the ending, which gives the singer time to breathe and plan the next line. In the replies, the quick singer often repeats the other's ending and then turns it to a new point.

## What is often mistaken

A common mistake is to hear all Xingguo songs as revolutionary songs. The Soviet period did produce many new texts, but the tunes and singing style come from an older mountain-song tradition. Nor is "ai ya lei" a casual filler: it is the opening signal, fixing the height and the breath of the whole song. Xingguo songs are also not the tea-picking tunes of southern Jiangxi opera, which have moved into theatre.
