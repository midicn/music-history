## 时间与地点

土家族聚居在湖南湘西、湖北恩施、重庆石柱、彭水、酉阳与贵州铜仁。清江、酉水、武陵山是主要分布区。土家语属汉藏语系藏缅语族，多数人已转用汉语，只有少数村寨还能说。摆手节在农历正月或三月举行，哭嫁在婚礼前夜，薅草锣鼓在夏季薅草时打。打溜子是土家族与周边汉族共享的锣鼓乐。

## 发生了什么

摆手歌是摆手舞的唱本，唱创世、迁徙、农事与爱情，由梯玛或歌师领唱，众人接腔。哭嫁歌由新娘与女伴唱，唱父母养育、姐妹离别与婚后生活，唱得越久越显情分。薅草锣鼓在集体薅草时由歌师击鼓敲锣，边打边唱，催工也记事。打溜子用马锣、大锣、头钹、二钹，四人合奏，段落有固定的锣鼓经。二十世纪后期，这些形式进入舞台与旅游演出，村里的仪式仍在进行。

## 音乐特征

摆手歌多用五声，句幅整齐，领唱与齐唱交替，脚步重拍与歌声同步。哭嫁歌用哭腔，句尾下滑带颤，歌词即兴，称呼与套语反复。薅草锣鼓的节奏紧，鼓点领、锣钹跟，唱词短。打溜子的音色靠四种铜响的对比，马锣尖、大锣沉、钹分头钹二钹，段落以溜子为单位连接。咚咚喹是小竹笛，音域窄，多用于儿歌与情歌。

## 听什么

先听哭嫁歌的句尾怎样下滑，再听称呼与套语怎样反复。essen-001137 与 essen-001167 标题写明土家，可听敬赛与丰收两种场合的句读。chinafolk-008305 与 chinafolk-008306 是哭嫁歌的不同段落，能听到哭腔与重复。打溜子的铜响在录音里不易分辨，可留意锣钹交替的密度。

## 常见误解

常见误解是把哭嫁当成真的哭闹，或把土家族音乐等同于摆手舞。哭嫁是有词有调的仪式歌，摆手歌、薅草锣鼓、打溜子各有体系。土家语流失不等于音乐消失，汉语唱词仍在村寨与舞台上传。也不是所有土家族地区都唱同样的调子，湘西、鄂西、渝东南各有差别。

## Time and place

The Tujia live in Xiangxi in Hunan, Enshi in Hubei, Shizhu, Pengshui and Youyang in Chongqing, and Tongren in Guizhou. Tujia belongs to the Tibeto-Burman branch of Sino-Tibetan; most people now speak Chinese, and only a few villages keep the language. The baishou festival falls in the first or third lunar month, wedding laments are sung the night before a wedding, and weeding gong-and-drum music is played in summer. Daliuzi percussion is shared with neighbouring Han communities.

## What happened

Baishou songs carry the baishou dance, telling creation, migration, farming and love, led by a ritual specialist or song master with a responding chorus. Wedding laments are sung by the bride and her companions, covering parental care, parting from sisters and married life; the longer the singing, the greater the affection shown. Weeding gong-and-drum music is led by a song master who beats a drum and gong, urging the work on and recording events. Daliuzi uses a small gong, large gong and two cymbals in a quartet. From the late twentieth century these forms entered stage and tourism, while village rites continue.

## Musical features

Baishou songs are mostly pentatonic with even phrase lengths, alternating lead and unison, the footfall matching the strong beat. Wedding laments use a crying tone with falling, trembling phrase ends, improvised words and repeated address formulas. Weeding music is tight, the drum leading and gongs following, with short sung lines. Daliuzi depends on the contrast of four bronze timbres, the small gong sharp, the large gong deep, the cymbals split between lead and answer, sections linked by named patterns. The dudongkui is a small bamboo flute with a narrow range, used for children's songs and love songs.

## What to listen for

Hear how a wedding lament falls at the phrase end, then how address formulas repeat. Essen-001137 and essen-001167 are explicitly Tujia, showing the phrasing of a respect song and a harvest song. Chinafolk-008305 and chinafolk-008306 are separate lament sections where the crying tone and repetition are audible. The bronze sound of daliuzi is hard to separate in a recording; listen for the density of gong and cymbal alternation.

## Common misunderstandings

Wedding laments are often taken as literal weeping, and Tujia music reduced to the baishou dance. The laments are ritual songs with words and tunes, while baishou songs, weeding music and daliuzi each form a system. Language loss does not mean music loss; Chinese-language verses still circulate in villages and on stage. Nor does every Tujia area sing the same tunes; Xiangxi, western Hubei and southeastern Chongqing differ.
