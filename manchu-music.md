## 时间与地点

满族分布在辽宁、吉林、黑龙江、北京、河北与内蒙古东部。辽宁新宾、岫岩、凤城，吉林伊通、九台，黑龙江宁安、富裕是满族聚居与自治地方。满语属阿尔泰语系满通古斯语族，使用满文，但日常多已转用汉语，能说满语的人很少。萨满祭祀在家族或村屯的堂子、庭院进行，背灯祭在夜间，祭天在院中立索伦杆。

## 发生了什么

满族音乐的主干是萨满祭祀。神歌由萨满与助手唱，击抓鼓、摇腰铃，请神、领神、送神按次序进行；背灯祭在熄灯后进行，唱词与鼓点都压低。八角鼓是带小钹的框架鼓，用于说唱与歌舞，清代北京把八角鼓与岔曲、子弟书连在一起。子弟书由八旗子弟创作，唱词是汉语，音乐吸收北方说唱。悠悠调是摇篮曲，母亲在摇车旁哼唱。二十世纪后期，萨满仪式一度中断，近年有家族恢复，神歌的完整唱本多靠老人记忆。

## 音乐特征

神歌的旋律窄，常在一个五度内重复，句尾下滑，鼓点与脚步对应。请神与送神的段落速度不同，请神慢，送神快。八角鼓的说唱用真声，句幅整齐，鼓点轻。悠悠调用轻声，节奏摇荡，句尾拖长。满族民歌留存不多，许多曲调与东北汉族民歌共享，但唱词与场合不同。

## 听什么

先听神歌的短句怎样重复，再听抓鼓与腰铃怎样把拍点压住。chinafolk-006839 是背灯调，chinafolk-006867 是请神，chinafolk-006858 是祭天，三首能听到仪式不同阶段的语气。chinafolk-006816 是悠摇车，听摇篮曲的晃动感。这些是学习区录音，不是仪式现场。

## 常见误解

常见误解是以为满族音乐已经消失，或把它等同于清代宫廷音乐。萨满神歌、八角鼓与悠悠调仍在家族与村屯中传，宫廷乐是另一套制度。也不是所有满族人都信萨满，仪式在部分家族恢复，另一些人只保留节庆与饮食习俗。子弟书用汉语唱，但它的创作群体与八旗生活密不可分。

## Time and place

The Manchu live in Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Beijing, Hebei and eastern Inner Mongolia. Xinbin, Xiuyan and Fengcheng in Liaoning, Yitong and Jiutai in Jilin, and Ning'an and Fuyu in Heilongjiang are centres of Manchu settlement and autonomy. Manchu belongs to the Tungusic branch of Altaic, but daily life has largely shifted to Chinese and few fluent speakers remain. Shamanic rites take place in a family shrine or courtyard, the back-lamp rite at night, and the heaven sacrifice before a pole in the yard.

## What happened

Manchu music is centred on shamanic ritual. Spirit songs are sung by the shaman and assistants with a hand drum and waist bells, in a sequence of inviting, receiving and sending off the spirits. The back-lamp rite is held in darkness, with lowered voices and drumming. The octagonal drum is a frame drum with small cymbals, used for narrative and dance; in Qing Beijing it joined the chaqu and zidishu traditions. Zidishu was created by banner literati, with Chinese words and northern narrative music. The youyou tune is a lullaby hummed beside a cradle. Ritual was interrupted in the late twentieth century and has been revived by some families, with texts resting on elders' memory.

## Musical features

Spirit songs move in a narrow range, often repeating within a fifth, with falling ends and drum strokes matching the steps. Inviting and sending-off sections differ in speed, the first slow and the second fast. Octagonal-drum narrative uses chest voice, even phrases and light strokes. The lullaby is soft, rocking in rhythm with drawn-out ends. Few Manchu folk songs survive; many tunes are shared with Han songs of the northeast, but words and occasions differ.

## What to listen for

Hear how short spirit phrases repeat, then how the hand drum and waist bells weight the pulse. Chinafolk-006839 is a back-lamp song, chinafolk-006867 is an invitation to the spirits, and chinafolk-006858 is a heaven sacrifice; the three show the tone of different ritual stages. Chinafolk-006816 is a lullaby, where the rocking feel is clear. These are study-zone recordings, not ritual field recordings.

## Common misunderstandings

Manchu music is often assumed to have vanished, or is equated with Qing court music. Shamanic songs, the octagonal drum and lullabies still pass through families and villages; court music was a separate institution. Nor are all Manchu people shamanists; ritual has been revived in some families while others keep only festival and food customs. Zidishu is sung in Chinese, but its makers and their banner life are inseparable from Manchu history.
