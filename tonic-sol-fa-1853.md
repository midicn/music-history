::: zh
五线谱要求先认线和间，再算音程。柯温换了一条路，用 do re mi 直接标出音级，只要知道主音在哪里，谁都能开口唱。

## 时间与地点

十九世纪四十至五十年代，伦敦与诺里奇。诺里奇的格洛弗在1830年代编出自己的唱名法，公理会牧师柯温在1840年代把它改写，1850年代定名为 tonic sol-fa 并开始大规模推广。哪一年算正式起点，各家说法不一。

## 发生了什么

这套体系用 do re mi fa sol la ti 七个音节代表音级，do 的位置随调移动，唱名本身就是音级，不必先读谱号。柯温把节奏也音节化，用 dash 与逗号标出长短，印成一种独立的记谱法，比五线谱便宜也更容易排字。教材按班级编写，教师先学后教，几周就能带出一支能唱分声部的合唱队。十九世纪后半叶，这套方法随教会与传教士进入印度、中国、非洲与太平洋岛屿，成为最早跨语种推广的识谱工具之一。

## 改变了什么

识谱的门槛降了下来，工人、儿童与女性合唱团不必先受多年器乐训练。廉价印刷让歌本可以人手一册，合唱运动因此有了群众基础。它也引起争论，专业音乐家担心首调唱名会削弱绝对音高与读谱能力，英国的音乐教育界为此争了几十年。柯达伊后来在匈牙利采用可动 do，把这条线索接到二十世纪。今天学校里仍在用的首调唱名，源头多半可以追到柯温。

## 听这个转折

先听《Amazing Grace》的新不列颠曲调，旋律以级进为主，用首调唱名唱一遍就能感到主音的位置。再听《Cwm Rhondda》，短小的音型反复出现，适合集体分声部，唱名让每个声部都知道自己站在第几级。最后听《Hyfrydol》，跳进不多，句尾落在主音或属音上，是视唱课本喜欢的形状。三首都是赞美诗，正是这套教学法最主要的教材。

::: en
Staff notation asks you to name lines and spaces before you can measure an interval. Curwen took another road, marking scale degrees directly with do re mi, so anyone who knows where the tonic lies can begin to sing.

## Time and place

Britain in the 1840s and 1850s, in London and Norwich. Sarah Ann Glover of Norwich devised her own sol-fa in the 1830s; the Congregational minister John Curwen rewrote it in the 1840s, settled on the name tonic sol-fa in the 1850s and began promoting it widely. Sources disagree on which year counts as the formal start.

## What happened

The system used seven syllables, do re mi fa sol la ti, for scale degrees, with do moving to the tonic of whatever key was in use. The syllables were the degrees themselves, so no clef had to be read first. Curwen also turned rhythm into syllables, using dashes and commas for length, and printed the result as an independent notation that was cheaper to set than a staff. Textbooks were organised by class, teachers were trained to train others, and within weeks a group could sing in parts. In the later nineteenth century the method travelled with churches and missionaries to India, China, Africa and the Pacific, one of the earliest notation systems promoted across languages.

## What changed

The threshold for reading music fell. Workers, children and women's choirs no longer needed years of instrumental training first. Cheap printing put a book in every hand, and the choral movement gained a mass base. It also provoked argument, since professional musicians feared that movable do would weaken absolute pitch and staff reading, a dispute that ran for decades in British music education. Kodaly later adopted movable do in Hungary, carrying the line into the twentieth century. Much of the movable-do teaching still used in schools traces back to Curwen.

## Listen for the turn

Begin with the New Britain tune of Amazing Grace, mostly stepwise, so that singing it in sol-fa immediately shows where the tonic sits. Then Cwm Rhondda, whose short figure repeats and suits part-singing, the syllables telling each voice which degree it holds. Finally Hyfrydol, with few leaps and phrases closing on tonic or dominant, the shape a sight-singing book prefers. All three are hymns, the core repertoire of this method.
