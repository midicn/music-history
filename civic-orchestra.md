十九世纪的乐团不再由宫廷发薪，而由一群买票的市民自己组织、自己管理。

## 时间与地点

1813 年，伦敦，一群演奏家成立爱乐协会，会员既是乐手也是股东，按章程投票选理事。1842 年，纽约爱乐乐团与维也纳爱乐乐团先后成立。三座城市分处不同国家，模式却相似，都是先有协会，再有固定的演出季与曲目库。

## 发生了什么

协会自己租音乐厅，卖年票与单场票，请独唱独奏，雇乐队。乐团还设基金与赞助人名单，亏损年份由理事补足，账目在年会上公布，乐季通常从秋到春，夏季休息。理事们决定曲目，常常把贝多芬、莫扎特、海顿放在中心，再配新作。伦敦的协会向贝多芬约了第九交响曲，也收藏手稿与分谱，建起自己的谱库，后来的乐团都照着这套做法攒家底。乐手按演出季领钱，不再是穿制服的仆人；指挥从首席或键盘手中分离出来，成为排练的组织者。乐队规模跟着厅的大小与票房走，木管、铜管与打击乐逐渐固定成后来的标准编制。

## 改变了什么

乐团成为常设的市民机构，有章程、有财务、有年度计划。曲目从“本周新作”转为可反复演奏的经典，音乐季围绕这些作品安排，听众可以逐年比较同一部交响曲的不同演法。票房与赞助替代了宫廷预算，乐团成为城市荣誉的一部分，火车站、音乐厅与报纸共同塑造它的公众形象。

## 听这个转折

先听莫扎特《第二十五交响曲》，乐队小，弦乐为主，力度变化节制，是宫廷规模的写法。再听贝多芬《第五交响曲》第一乐章，全奏的爆发、定音鼓的重复敲击，音乐按大厅的尺度写。最后听贝多芬《小提琴协奏曲》，独奏家站在乐队前面，身份已经是公众明星。转折前，音乐为府邸的耳朵写；转折后，它为几百个陌生人写。注意定音鼓与低音弦乐的作用，它们在宫廷编制里往往只是陪衬。

In the nineteenth century the orchestra stopped drawing a court salary and was organised and managed by ticket-buying citizens.

## Time and place

London, 1813, where a group of players founded the Philharmonic Society, its members both performers and shareholders, electing directors by written rules. In 1842 the New York Philharmonic and the Vienna Philharmonic followed. Three cities in different countries adopted one pattern: a society first, then a fixed season and a repertory library.

## What happened

The society rented a hall, sold season and single tickets, engaged soloists and paid the band. It kept a fund and a list of patrons, directors covering a loss and publishing the accounts at the annual meeting. Directors chose programmes, usually placing Beethoven, Mozart and Haydn at the centre with newer works alongside. The London society commissioned Beethoven's Ninth and collected manuscripts and parts for its own library, a habit later orchestras followed when building their archives. Players were paid by the season rather than serving in livery, and the conductor separated from the first violin or keyboard to become the organiser of rehearsals. The band grew with hall size and ticket income, its woodwind, brass and percussion settling into the standard layout.

## What changed

The orchestra became a permanent civic institution with statutes, accounts and an annual plan. Repertory shifted from this week's novelty to classics that could be played again and again, and seasons were built around them, so listeners could compare one reading of a symphony with the next. Ticket income and patronage replaced the court budget, and the orchestra became part of civic pride, its public image shaped by stations, halls and newspapers.

## Listen for the turn

Begin with Mozart's Symphony No. 25, a small band led by strings with restrained dynamic change, written to court scale. Then the first movement of Beethoven's Fifth, with its full tutti explosions and repeated timpani strokes, music measured to a hall. Finally Beethoven's Violin Concerto, the soloist standing before the orchestra, already a public celebrity. Before the turn, music was written for a household's ear; afterwards, for several hundred strangers. Notice the timpani and low strings, often mere support in a court band.
