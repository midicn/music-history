::: zh
战争需要声音。行军要节拍，动员要歌，悼念要安静。

## 问题

音乐在战争里是工具还是见证？它服务于权力，也能保存个人经验。同一首曲子，在前线与在纪念碑前可能完全不同。

## 历史脉络

军乐队随军队行进，进行曲把步伐变成集体动作。美国内战时期《共和国战歌》从废奴歌曲变成联邦军歌。肖邦的军队波罗乃兹在民族起义语境里被反复演奏。二十世纪，无线电、唱片与电影让战争音乐跨越前线；肖斯塔科维奇在围城中写交响曲，布列顿用安魂曲追问战争。霍斯特的军乐组曲则把民歌带进军营。

## 关键转折

转折在技术。广播与录音让一首歌同时抵达千万人，宣传效率提高；战后，音乐又从动员转向记忆，纪念碑、纪念仪式与安魂曲把战争变成可听的公共历史。

## 听什么

先听《共和国战歌》的铜管式推进，再听柴可夫斯基军队进行曲的仪式感，最后听霍斯特军乐组曲里的民歌段落，注意军营与乡土如何并置。

::: en
War needs sound. Marching needs a beat, mobilisation a song, mourning silence.

## The question

Is music in war a tool or a witness? It serves power, and it can preserve private experience.

## Historical arc

Military bands marched with armies, and marches turned steps into collective motion. During the American Civil War, The Battle Hymn of the Republic moved from an abolitionist song to a Union marching song. Chopin's Military Polonaise was played in the context of national uprising. In the twentieth century radio, records and film carried war music across the front. Shostakovich wrote a symphony inside a besieged city, and Britten used a requiem to question war. Holst's suite brought English folk song into barracks.

## The turning point

Radio and recording let one song reach millions at once and made propaganda more efficient. After the war, music shifted from mobilisation to memory; monuments and requiems turned war into audible public history.

## What to listen for

Hear the brass-like drive of The Battle Hymn of the Republic, the ceremonial tread of Tchaikovsky's Military March, and the folk-song passage in Holst's suite.
