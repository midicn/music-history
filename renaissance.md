::: zh
文艺复兴不只是复古，而是欧洲重新分配知识、金钱与权力的过程。音乐上，作曲家开始有意识地塑造和声、模仿与歌词的关系，印刷术让作品走出宫廷，传到欧洲各地。

## 时代背景

15世纪的城市国家、宫廷与教廷构成赞助网络；美第奇家族、教皇礼拜堂与帝国宫廷竞相延揽歌手和作曲家。15世纪中叶活字印刷成熟，1501年威尼斯出现专门印刷乐谱的工坊，作品可以大量复制。16世纪宗教改革削弱教会统一性，特伦托会议讨论教堂音乐的可懂度，世俗牧歌与器乐则在宫廷与贵族间流行。

## 音乐特征

文艺复兴复调追求声部平等：每个声部都有旋律线，却在协和音程上汇合。模仿让同一动机在各声部依次出现，形成建筑般的整体；三度、六度从不完全协和变成常规音响，和声听感比中世纪更饱满。作曲家会用上行表达上升、用快速音型表达奔跑，这种绘词法在牧歌里最明显。终止式逐渐标准化，为后来的调性语言铺路。

## 体裁与形式

弥撒仍是最大型的宗教体裁，常以一首现成旋律作固定声部；经文歌把宗教文本与政治或纪念内容并置。世俗体裁包括法语尚松、意大利牧歌与弗罗托拉、西班牙维良西科，以及英国的牧歌与琉特琴歌曲。器乐从声乐中独立，出现里切卡尔、坎佐纳与舞曲组曲。

## 乐器与编制

声乐仍居中心，但乐器组合已经制度化。维奥尔琴族按尺寸成套，琉特琴用于独奏与伴奏，竖笛、克鲁姆管、肖姆管、萨克布号与科内特管组成高声部与低声部的乐队。教堂管风琴与早期羽管键琴承担固定声部。乐器常与声部齐奏或替换声部，音色因此成为作曲考虑的一部分。

## 代表作曲家

- 若斯坎·德普雷（1450–1521）：法-佛兰德乐派代表，模仿与和声控制被视为文艺复兴复调的典范。
- 帕莱斯特里纳（1525–1594）：罗马乐派，声部进行平滑、不协和音受严格准备，适合教堂的清晰吐字。
- 奥兰多·迪·拉索（1532–1594）：作品横跨宗教与世俗，牧歌与经文歌都带强烈的戏剧感。
- 威廉·伯德（1540–1623）：英国天主教作曲家，把复调技术带进礼拜音乐与键盘音乐。

## 听什么

听牧歌时，先抓每个声部的进入：同一个动机从高到低轮流出现，像几个人接同一句话。再听弥撒的羔羊经：声部减少时，和声变得更透明，终止式把段落分开。最后注意歌词重音与音乐重音是否重合，这是文艺复兴作曲家最在意的手艺。

## 中国对照

明代中国的声音世界由宫廷雅乐、戏曲与文人琴乐共同构成。昆山腔在16世纪经魏良辅改良，水磨调讲究字声与气息；工尺谱在民间与戏曲中广泛使用；古琴谱以减字谱记录指法。中西都在16世纪扩大音乐的传播，但欧洲靠印刷，中国更多靠师徒与班社。

::: en
The Renaissance was more than a revival. It reallocated knowledge, money and power, and music felt it: composers shaped harmony, imitation and word setting, while printing carried songs beyond the court.

## The world behind the music

City-states, courts and the papacy formed a patronage network; the Medici, papal chapel and imperial courts competed for singers and composers. Movable type spread, and by 1501 Venice had a music-printing workshop, so works could be copied. The Reformation divided the church, the Council of Trent debated intelligibility in sacred music; madrigals and instrumental music flourished in courts.

## What the music does

Renaissance polyphony seeks equal voices: each has its line, yet all meet on consonance. Imitation lets one motive pass from voice to voice and builds an architectural whole; thirds and sixths become normal sonorities, making the harmony fuller than medieval music. Composers set rising lines for ascent and quick figures for running, a word-painting vivid in the madrigal. Cadences grow standard and prepare the later tonal language.

## Genres and forms

The Mass remains the largest sacred genre, often built on a borrowed melody as cantus firmus; the motet sets sacred texts beside political or commemorative ones. Secular genres include the French chanson, the Italian madrigal and frottola, the Spanish villancico, and the English madrigal and lute song. Instrumental music gains independence in the ricercar, canzona and dance suite.

## Instruments and forces

Voices remain central, but instrumental ensembles are now organised. Viols are made in consorts by size, the lute serves solo and accompaniment, and recorders, crumhorns, shawms, sackbuts and cornettos form high and low bands. Church organ and the early harpsichord supply the fixed voice. Instruments double or replace voices, so timbre becomes part of composition.

## Composers

- Josquin des Prez (1450–1521): a leading Franco-Flemish composer; his control of imitation and harmony became a model for Renaissance polyphony.
- Palestrina (1525–1594): Roman school; smooth voice-leading and carefully prepared dissonance keep the words clear in church.
- Orlando di Lasso (1532–1594): his output spans sacred and secular, with madrigals and motets full of dramatic gesture.
- William Byrd (1540–1623): English Catholic composer who brought polyphonic craft to liturgy and keyboard music.

## What to listen for

In a madrigal, follow the entries: one motive passes from high to low like several people taking up the same sentence. In the Agnus Dei of a Mass, hear the texture thin and the harmony become transparent as voices drop out, with cadences marking the sections. Then check whether word stress and musical stress coincide; that craft mattered more than any single chord.

## China, at the same time

Ming China's sound world combined court ritual music, opera and literati qin music. Kunshan singing was reformed by Wei Liangfu in the sixteenth century, its water-polished style shaping words and breath; gongche notation spread through folk and operatic circles, while qin notation recorded fingerings. Both places widened musical circulation, but Europe used print while China relied more on teachers and troupes.
