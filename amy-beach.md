## 出身与师承

**艾米·比奇**（1867–1944）生于新罕布什尔州的亨尼克尔，幼年随家迁往波士顿。她跟佩拉博与鲍曼学钢琴，作曲基本自学，读柏辽兹与凯鲁比尼的配器与对位教材。1885 年她与医生比奇结婚，婚后大幅减少公开演出；丈夫去世后她重新巡演，1910 年代在欧洲演出自己的作品，1944 年在纽约去世。

## 时代与位置

比奇是**美国十九世纪末「波士顿古典」圈子的代表**，也是第一个写出交响曲的美国女性。1896 年《盖尔交响曲》由波士顿交响乐团首演，让她一夜成名。她的语言接近勃拉姆斯与柴可夫斯基，讲究发展与配器；钢琴作品则多为性格小品，标题清楚地指向风景、童话与地方色彩。唱片目录里她常只标姓氏 Beach，查找时需要留心。

## 先听什么

先听**小品的画面**。《四首素描》Op.15 各写秋日、幻影、梦与萤火，和声在每首里换一套颜色，可以当作入门。再听**固定音型的冷**，《爱斯基摩人》Op.64 用重复的低音与半音碰撞制造北方的空气，听它怎样不靠旋律而靠织体说话。然后听两只手的分工，《静水之畔》Op.114 左手琶音几乎不停，右手旋律却要保持独立，听两条线怎样互不干扰。最后听歌曲的开头，《一年之春》Op.44 No.1 的钢琴前奏先定下轻快的语气，人声进来时只是接着走。

## 作品与写法

- 钢琴，《四首素描》Op.15、《爱斯基摩人》Op.64、《静水之畔》Op.114
- 大型作品，《盖尔交响曲》Op.32、钢琴协奏曲 Op.45
- 其他，《巴尔干主题变奏》Op.60、歌曲《一年之春》Op.44 No.1

## 影响与误读

比奇的意义有两层。她用交响曲证明美国女性可以写大结构，也用大量小品证明小体裁同样需要手艺；她晚年也支持女性音乐家的组织，为后来的创作者争到可见的位置。她的音乐今天被重新录音，听者才发现十九世纪的美国并不只有模仿欧洲的一端。

## Training and teachers

**Amy Beach** (1867–1944) was born in Henniker, New Hampshire, and moved to Boston as a child. She studied piano with Perabo and Baermann and taught herself composition from treatises on orchestration and counterpoint by Berlioz and Cherubini. In 1885 she married Dr Henry Beach and largely withdrew from public performance; after his death she toured again, played her own music in Europe in the 1910s, and died in New York in 1944.

## His place

Beach represents the **Boston classical circle of the late nineteenth century** and was the first American woman to compose a symphony. The Gaelic Symphony, premiered by the Boston Symphony in 1896, made her name at once. Her language is close to Brahms and Tchaikovsky, built on development and orchestration; her piano music is mostly character pieces with titles pointing to landscape, fairy tale and local colour. Record catalogues often list her under the surname Beach alone, which is worth remembering when searching.

## Where to start

Listen first for **pictures in miniature**. The four Sketches Op.15 describe autumn, phantoms, a dream and fireflies, each with its own harmonic colour, and they make a good entry point. Then hear **cold through a fixed figure**: Eskimos Op.64 builds northern air from repeating bass notes and semitone clashes, speaking through texture rather than tune. Then listen to the division of hands. By the Still Waters Op.114 keeps the left hand arpeggiating almost without pause while the right stays independent, the two lines never crowding each other. Finally take the opening of a song: the piano prelude of The Year's at the Spring Op.44 No.1 sets a light mood before the voice simply continues it.

## Works and craft

- Piano: Four Sketches Op.15; Eskimos Op.64; By the Still Waters Op.114
- Large works: Gaelic Symphony Op.32; Piano Concerto Op.45
- Other: Variations on Balkan Themes Op.60; the song The Year's at the Spring Op.44 No.1

## Influence and misreadings

Beach matters on two levels. She proved with a symphony that an American woman could build large forms, and her many miniatures show that small genres demand craft as well; in her later years she also supported organisations for women musicians and won visibility for those who came after. New recordings have shown that nineteenth-century American music was not only a copy of Europe.
