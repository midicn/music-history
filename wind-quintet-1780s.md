::: zh
1780年代的巴黎，五种发音方式完全不同的管乐器被写进同一部作品。管乐五重奏不是某位大师的宣言，而是出版市场、业余管乐手与作曲家一起试出来的编制。

## 年代与城市

1780年代，巴黎。意大利作曲家坎比尼在此出版多套管乐五重奏，长笛、双簧管、单簧管、圆号、大管各一件。三十年后，赖夏在巴黎与维也纳写下二十四首同类作品，把编制与写法彻底固定下来。

## 当时发生了什么

在此之前，管乐合奏多为户外或礼仪用的"哈莫尼音乐"，以成对的双管编制为主，音乐服从场面。坎比尼把五种音色不同、音量不齐的乐器写成对话：长笛与双簧管轮流领奏，单簧管填中声部，圆号给和声支点，大管做低音与节奏。写作的难点全在平衡，五种乐器的强弱与发音方式都不一样，作曲家必须重新分配角色，让弱的乐器在不被淹没的位置上说话。五件乐器里没有一件能长期压过其他四件。当时的管乐手多是宫廷或剧院的在职乐师，五重奏让他们下班后也有自己的合奏。这种编制后来被直接称为木管五重奏。

## 后来变了什么

管乐五重奏成为室内乐的一支独立体裁，不再只是改编歌剧咏叹调的载体。管乐手第一次拥有不依赖弦乐的曲目，听众也学会了"五种音色互相让位"的听法。后来的管乐写作，从赖夏到二十世纪的法国学派，都以这套编制为默认配置。

## 听这一刻

先听坎比尼《降B大调第一五重奏》的开头，长笛与双簧管交替，圆号在句尾托底；再听《D小调第二五重奏》，小调让单簧管的中声部变厚；最后听贝多芬那首管乐五重奏 Hess 19，同一个编制在更重的和声里如何保持清晰。库中都是合奏录音，注意谁在领、谁在让。

In Paris in the 1780s, five wind instruments with very different ways of speaking were written into the same work. The wind quintet was not one master's manifesto but a scoring worked out by publishers, amateur players and composers together.

## Year and city

Paris, in the 1780s. The Italian composer Giuseppe Cambini published several sets of wind quintets there, for flute, oboe, clarinet, horn and bassoon. Thirty years later Anton Reicha wrote twenty-four more in Paris and Vienna and fixed the scoring and the style.

## What happened then

Before this, wind ensembles mostly played Harmoniemusik for outdoor or ceremonial use, usually in paired two-part scoring, with the music serving the occasion. Cambini wrote the five colours as a conversation: flute and oboe take turns leading, clarinet fills the middle, horn anchors the harmony, bassoon supplies bass and rhythm. The difficulty is balance, since the instruments differ in volume and attack, so the composer must redistribute roles and place the weaker instruments where they can be heard. Most players were court or theatre musicians, and the quintet gave them music for their own evenings.

## What changed after

The wind quintet became an independent chamber genre rather than a vehicle for operatic arrangements. Wind players gained repertory that did not depend on strings, and listeners learned to hear five colours making room for each other. Later writing for the medium, from Reicha to the twentieth-century French school, treats this scoring as the default.

## Listen for this moment

Begin with the opening of Cambini's Quintet No. 1 in B flat, flute and oboe alternating while the horn supports the cadence. Then the Quintet No. 2 in D minor, where the minor key thickens the clarinet's middle voice. Finally Beethoven's early Wind Quintet Hess 19, the same group keeping clarity under heavier harmony. All are ensemble recordings; notice who leads and who yields.
