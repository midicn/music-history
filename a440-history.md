::: zh
乐团调音时常先给出一个A，但合奏需要约定的是这个A对应的实际频率。A4=440 Hz是国际标准参考，不是自然界唯一正确的音乐音高。

## 从地方约定到标准文件

历史上的音高会随地区、场所、乐器和时期改变，不能只用作曲家的国籍或作品年代判断。1859年法国的A=435和1939年伦敦会议的A=440，是标准化过程中的不同节点。

ISO/R 16-1955是1955年的推荐文件。1975年发布的ISO 16以技术内容相同的国际标准取代该推荐文件，明确标准A为440 Hz；不能把1955年仅写成“立项”，也不能认为1975年所有乐团才开始用440。

## 标准不等于每场演出的实际音高

演出仍须按乐团、乐器和曲目事先协商。今天一些古乐演出采用A约415 Hz，但415不是全部巴洛克音乐、全部地区或全部古乐团唯一的历史音高。固定音高的历史乐器、制作与合奏的便利，也会影响现代演出的选择。不能把“柏林”“维也纳”或“古乐”简单绑定成一个永远不变的频率。

## 听例的边界

本页曲目可帮助辨认不同年代的音乐写作，不能证明当年演出采用了哪个标准音高。MIDI文件和当前音源的回放设置也不等于历史录音；要比较实际音高，应查录音说明、乐器资料和测量条件。音高基准与十二平均律等音律方案是两个问题：前者确定参考频率，后者决定各音之间的关系。

::: en
An orchestra often begins tuning with an A, but players must agree on its actual frequency. A4=440 Hz is an international reference, not nature’s only correct musical pitch.

## From local choices to a standard

Historical pitch varied with region, venue, instrument and period. A composer’s nationality or a work’s date cannot establish it. France’s A=435 in 1859 and the London conference’s A=440 in 1939 were separate steps toward standardization.

ISO/R 16-1955 was a recommendation issued in 1955. ISO 16, published in 1975, replaced it with a technically identical international standard specifying 440 Hz. Thus 1955 was not merely a project start, and orchestras did not all begin using 440 in 1975.

## Reference versus performance practice

Ensembles still agree on pitch for their instruments and repertoire. Some early-music performances use about A415, but this is not the single historical pitch of every Baroque region or ensemble. Fixed-pitch instruments and practical ensemble choices matter too. A city or the label “early music” should not be assigned one permanently fixed frequency.

## Limits of the examples

These works illustrate musical writing across periods, not the tuning of their original performances. MIDI playback through the current sound source is not a historical recording. Investigate pitch using recording notes, instrument evidence and measurement conditions. Pitch reference and temperament are separate: one sets a reference frequency, the other the relationships between notes.
