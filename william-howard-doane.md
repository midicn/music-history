## 出身与师承

**威廉·霍华德·多恩**（1832–1915）生于康涅狄格州普雷斯顿，成年后定居辛辛那提，经营木工机械厂，是一位成功的工业家。他同时也是教会的主日学校长、唱诗班组织者与业余作曲家，一生写下数百首赞美诗曲调，其中许多由范妮·克罗斯比作词，如《To God Be the Glory》《Pass Me Not, O Gentle Savior》《Safe in the Arms of Jesus》。他还出资支持教会学校，晚年把大量精力放在宗教音乐出版上。

## 时代与位置

多恩的写作年代正是美国福音诗歌的黄金期：主日学、帐篷布道会与城市教会都需要大量新歌，曲调要简单、副歌要好记、歌词要直接。他不是职业作曲家，也不追求个人风格，而是**为现成的歌词提供合身的旋律**。这种合作关系在十九世纪很常见：词人写词，曲调作者按音节与重音配曲，出版人再把它们编进歌本。他的曲调大多先有词，再按音节配旋律，因此句读清楚、少有装饰。听他的作品，等于听一整套为会众设计的写作规范。

## 先听什么

先听**副歌的第一句**。《To God Be the Glory》的副歌以“Praise the Lord”向上冲，音高与节奏同时打开，像一句欢呼；主歌则平稳叙述，两者分工清楚。再听**重复怎样变成恳求**。《Pass Me Not, O Gentle Savior》主歌反复唱同一句请求，旋律每次回到相近的音，和声却稍微推进，重复不是原地打转，而是越求越急。最后听安静的表达。《More Love to Thee》旋律多向下走，句子短，几乎没有高潮，听它怎样用克制写出虔诚。三首中两首是声乐谱，一首是管风琴改编。

## 作品与写法

- 曲调：《To God Be the Glory》《Pass Me Not, O Gentle Savior》《More Love to Thee》《Rescue the Perishing》
- 其他：《Safe in the Arms of Jesus》《Near the Cross》
- 编集：主日学与福音诗歌集

## 影响与误读

多恩的曲调通过克罗斯比的歌词与大规模的歌本发行进入美国教会生活，至今仍在礼拜中使用。他的例子说明，十九世纪美国宗教音乐的生产是一条词、曲、出版分工的流水线；他既是工厂主也是这条流水线上的一环。他的作品价值在于合用：旋律不抢歌词，会众一开口就能找到音。他活到八十多岁，见证了这套歌本工业的顶峰。

## Training and teachers

**William Howard Doane** (1832–1915) was born in Preston, Connecticut, and settled in Cincinnati, where he ran a woodworking machinery business and became a successful industrialist. He was also a Sunday-school superintendent, choir organiser and amateur composer who wrote hundreds of hymn tunes, many of them set to words by Fanny Crosby, including To God Be the Glory, Pass Me Not, O Gentle Savior and Safe in the Arms of Jesus. He gave money to church schools and later devoted much energy to religious music publishing.

## His place

Doane wrote in the golden age of the American gospel song, when Sunday schools, revivals and city churches needed new songs: simple tunes, memorable refrains, direct words. He was not a professional composer and did not chase a style; he **provided melodies that fitted words already written**. The partnership was typical of the century: a text writer supplied verses, a tune writer matched syllables and accents, a publisher placed the result in a hymnal. To hear him is to hear conventions designed for congregations.

## Where to start

Hear first **the opening of the refrain**. In To God Be the Glory the refrain launches upward on "Praise the Lord", pitch and rhythm opening together like a shout, while the verse narrates calmly; the division of labour is clear. Then hear **how repetition becomes pleading**. Pass Me Not, O Gentle Savior repeats the same request in the verse, the melody returning to nearby notes while the harmony pushes slightly forward; the repetition is not standing still but growing more urgent. Finally hear quiet devotion. More Love to Thee moves mostly downward in short phrases with almost no climax; listen for restraint used as devotion. Two are vocal, one an organ arrangement.

## Works and craft

- Tunes: To God Be the Glory, Pass Me Not, O Gentle Savior, More Love to Thee, Rescue the Perishing
- Others: Safe in the Arms of Jesus, Near the Cross
- Editions: Sunday-school and gospel hymnals

## Influence and misreadings

Doane's tunes entered American church life through Crosby's words and hymnal distribution, and are still sung. His career shows that nineteenth-century American religious music ran on a division of labour between words, tune and publisher, and he was one link in that line as well as a factory owner. The value of his work is fitness for use: the tune never fights the words, and a congregation finds its pitch at once.
