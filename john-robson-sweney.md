## 生平一笔

**约翰·罗布森·斯威尼**（1837–1899）生于宾夕法尼亚州，长期在教会与营地聚会中担任音乐指导与领唱，也做音乐教师。他在宾夕法尼亚的学校与教会里教唱，熟悉没有受过训练的声音。他与威廉·柯克帕特里克等人合作编过多部福音诗歌集，自己写下大量曲调，《Beulah Land》《The Everlasting Arms》《Rock in the Desert》是其中传唱较广的几首。1899 年他在宾夕法尼亚去世。

## 他生活的城市

十九世纪后期，美国营地聚会与主日学运动需要大量新歌，曲调作者的任务很具体：旋律要在露天、无伴奏或只有簧风琴的条件下唱得起来，副歌要让人群在第二遍就能加入。斯威尼就是这类作者中的熟练工。他不追求个人风格，作品也很少进入音乐会曲目，**但他写的歌在当时的教会生活里出现频率极高**。听他的作品，最好把它放回帐篷、长椅与夏日午后的场景。

## 听什么

先听**副歌的易唱性**。《Beulah Land》的副歌音域窄、句子短，重复多，旋律几乎在几个音之间来回，这种写法不是简单，而是为人群设计。再听**低音怎样表示依靠**。《The Everlasting Arms》的低音持续、和声保守，很少离开主调，听“永久的膀臂”这个意象怎样被低音托住。最后听一个小跳进的作用。《Rock in the Desert》主歌平缓，副歌用一个向上的跳进把“荒凉”推开，随后又落回稳定的音；跳进不大，但在会众音域里刚好够用。三首都是管风琴改编，原版通常是人声加簧风琴。

## 代表作品

- 歌曲：《Beulah Land》《The Everlasting Arms》《Rock in the Desert》
- 其他：《Showers of Blessing》等福音诗歌的曲调与编集
- 编集：与柯克帕特里克等人合作的福音诗歌集

## 留下的痕迹

斯威尼的歌没有进入音乐史的主线，却通过歌本、营地聚会与后来的乡村教会传了很久；《Beulah Land》至今仍在一些教会唱。他的工作说明福音诗歌是一种集体工艺：词、曲、编集、领唱各有人负责，目标是让一群人当场开口。研究美国宗教音乐，绕不开这类“不著名”的作者。今天的营地聚会传统里，仍能听到他的旋律。

## A life in brief

**John Robson Sweney** (1837–1899) was born in Pennsylvania and worked as a music teacher and song leader at churches and camp meetings. He compiled gospel songbooks with William J. Kirkpatrick and others and wrote many tunes, including Beulah Land, The Everlasting Arms and Rock in the Desert. He died there in 1899.

## The city he worked in

In the later nineteenth century American camp meetings and the Sunday-school movement needed a constant supply of new songs, and the tune writer's task was concrete: the melody had to work outdoors, with a reed organ at most, and the refrain had to let a crowd join at the second verse. Sweney was skilled in that trade. He did not chase a personal style, and his pieces rarely reach the concert hall, **yet his songs were heard constantly in the church life of his time**. Hear them in their setting: a tent, benches, a summer afternoon.

## What to hear

Hear first **how singable the refrain is**. In Beulah Land the refrain has a narrow range, short phrases and much repetition, the melody circling a few notes. That is not simplicity for its own sake but design for a crowd. Then hear **how the bass carries reliance**. The Everlasting Arms keeps a sustained bass and conservative harmony that rarely leaves the tonic, so the image of everlasting arms is supported from below. Finally hear what one leap can do. Rock in the Desert stays calm in the verse; the refrain takes one upward leap to push the desert away, then settles back. The leap is small but fits a congregation's range. All three tracks are organ arrangements; the originals are usually voice and reed organ.

## Key works

- Songs: Beulah Land, The Everlasting Arms, Rock in the Desert
- Others: tunes and arrangements in gospel collections, including Showers of Blessing
- Collections: gospel songbooks compiled with Kirkpatrick and others

## What remains

Sweney's songs never entered the main narrative of music history, yet they travelled for decades through songbooks, camp meetings and rural churches; Beulah Land is still sung in some congregations. His work shows that gospel song was a collective craft: words, tune, compilation and song leading each had a specialist, and the goal was to make a room sing. Any account of American religious music must include them.
