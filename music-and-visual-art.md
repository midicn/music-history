## 要回答的问题

音乐听不见画，画也看不见声音。但作曲家不断从绘画、建筑与舞台设计借结构。音乐与视觉艺术之间，究竟是谁在翻译谁？

## 时间线

宫廷节庆、歌剧与芭蕾长期把音乐、布景和舞蹈结合在同一场演出中。19世纪标题音乐也会用文字、人物或图像提示器乐的构思，例如穆索尔斯基《图画展览会》与哈特曼纪念画展的联系。另一些作品使用视觉艺术的词汇作为标题，并不逐项描摹一幅画。比较这些关系时，应先区分确有来源的作品联系与听者自行产生的画面联想。

## 典型案例

《图画展览会》的“漫步”在画作之间移动，钢琴变成导览；舒曼《狂欢节》用音符拼出人物与面具，像一组声音肖像；德彪西《版画集》的“塔”用五声音阶与钟声写东方图像。三例分别对应展览、肖像与版画。

## 听这几处

听“漫步”的旋律是否像脚步；听《狂欢节》的段落是否突然换脸；听“塔”的低音是否像钟声在水面散开。

## What has to be answered

Music cannot see a painting, and a painting cannot hear sound. Yet composers keep borrowing structure from painting, architecture and stage design. Between music and the visual arts, who is translating whom?

## On the timeline

Court festivities, opera and ballet have long combined music, scenery and dance. Nineteenth-century programme music could also use words, characters or images to guide instrumental conception, as in the relationship between Mussorgsky’s Pictures at an Exhibition and the Hartmann memorial exhibition. Other works borrow visual-art vocabulary for their titles without describing a specific picture. Distinguish a documented connection from imagery supplied by the listener.

## Typical cases

The Promenade of Pictures moves between paintings, with the piano as guide. Schumann's Carnaval builds characters and masks from notes, like a set of sound portraits. The Pagodes of Estampes uses pentatonic figures and bells for an imagined East. Exhibition, portrait and print, one case each.

## Listen to these

Hear whether the Promenade melody sounds like footsteps. Hear whether the sections of Carnaval change face without warning. Hear whether the bass of Pagodes spreads like a bell across water.
