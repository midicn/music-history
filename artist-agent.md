::: zh
十九世纪中叶起，演奏家的名声不再只靠琴房，而由经纪人、海报与火车时刻表一起安排。

## 事情发生在哪一年

1840 年代，李斯特在欧洲各地巡演，行程由代理人分段安排。1850 年，林德在美国的巡演由巴纳姆经营，宣传先于音乐。二十世纪初，贾德森在纽约同时管理乐团与独奏家，把经纪做成常设机构。铁路与轮船把巡演半径扩大，港口城市成为必经站。

## 事情经过

巡演要订音乐厅、运钢琴、订旅馆、印海报、卖票、分成。代理人按合同抽成，经理人承担票房风险，也负责把演奏家包装成新闻。合同按城市与场次签，独家代理的范围越划越大，违约与分成比例都写进条款。经纪人还要处理海关、乐器运输与当地乐队的排练。巡演城市按铁路线排列，港口与温泉城是热门站。曲目按观众口味安排，上半场严肃，下半场炫技，终场加演。琴厂把三角琴运到各地，或指定当地代理商提供，乐器本身也成为广告位。报纸评论与海报互相配合，名声在几周内被制造出来。

## 留下的变化

演奏生涯变成有合同、有分成、有区域划分的生意。经纪人站在演奏家与音乐厅之间，决定谁能进哪个城市、演什么曲目。能卖票的体裁被放大，狂想曲、歌剧幻想曲与加演小品成为巡演标配，严肃作品反而要靠在节目单里争取位置。明星制度由此成形，后来的广播与唱片只是把同一套机制放大。

## 听一段转折

先听李斯特《匈牙利狂想曲》第二首，慢段先摆姿态，快段一路加速，结尾留出鼓掌的空档。再听塔尔贝格《塞米拉米德幻想曲》，歌剧旋律被拆开重组，技巧段落一段比一段难，目的就是让观众站起来。最后听舒曼据帕格尼尼随想曲写的练习曲，把巡演明星的绝技搬进练习曲。转折前，演奏家靠宫廷职位或沙龙口碑；转折后，他们靠经理人、票房与报纸。听的时候注意加演曲往往比正式曲目更简单直接，那是给掌声留的接口。

From the mid-nineteenth century a performer's fame was arranged not only in the practice room but by agents, posters and railway timetables.

## The year it happened

Liszt toured Europe in the 1840s with agents booking him city by city. In 1850 Lind's American tour was run by Barnum, with publicity ahead of the music. In the early twentieth century Judson managed orchestras and soloists in New York, turning management into a permanent institution. Railways and steamships widened the circuit, and port cities became obligatory stops.

## The sequence of events

A tour meant booking halls, moving pianos, reserving hotels, printing posters, selling tickets and dividing the takings. Agents took a percentage by contract, impresarios carried the box-office risk and also packaged the performer as news. Contracts were signed city by city, with exclusive territory growing wider and clauses covering default and shares; the agent also handled customs, instrument transport and rehearsal with local orchestras. Touring cities were ordered along railway lines, ports and spa towns being favoured stops. Programmes followed audience taste, a serious first half, a virtuoso second, encores at the end. Piano makers shipped grands or appointed local agents to supply them, and the instrument itself became advertising space. Reviews and posters worked together, and a reputation could be manufactured within weeks.

## What it left behind

A performing career became a business with contracts, shares and territories. The agent stood between artist and hall, deciding who reached which city and what they played. Genres that sold were magnified, so rhapsodies, opera fantasies and encore pieces became touring stock, while serious works had to fight for a place on the programme. The star system took shape, and radio and records later amplified the same machinery.

## Listen for the turn

Begin with Liszt's Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2, the slow section striking a pose, the fast one accelerating to the end, with a silence left for applause. Then Thalberg's Semiramide fantasy, opera tunes broken up and rebuilt, each display harder than the last, designed to bring an audience to its feet. Finally Schumann's studies on Paganini caprices, which move a touring star's tricks into the study. Before the turn performers lived on court posts or salon reputations; afterwards on managers, takings and newspapers. Notice that an encore is usually simpler and more direct than the official programme, an interface built for applause.
