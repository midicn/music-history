## 他是谁

**菲利普·保罗·布利斯**（1838–1876）生于宾夕法尼亚州罗马，做过教师，1864 年迁到芝加哥，先做音乐出版与教唱，后来成为布道会的歌唱传道人。他从小在教会唱诗环境里长大，也教过唱歌学校。他写词也写曲，编过《Gospel Songs》等歌本，《Let the Lower Lights Be Burning》《Wonderful Words of Life》《Hallelujah! What a Savior》都在短短几年内传开。1876 年 12 月，他与妻子在俄亥俄州阿什塔比拉的火车事故中遇难，年仅三十八岁。

## 时代与位置

布利斯活动的年代，美国福音诗歌正从布道会走向家庭与主日学。他的歌有两个特点：一是**独唱与副歌的分工清楚**，主歌叙述、副歌邀请；二是**和声不复杂但转调果断**，常用一次上行或一次转调把情绪推到位。他既是词人也是曲作者，这让他能把句读与旋律一起考虑。他与桑基、穆迪那一圈人共享同一个市场与同一批歌本，作品属于教会音乐，不是音乐会曲目。

## 听什么

先听**副歌怎样给出答复**。《It Is Well with My Soul》主歌有疑问、有波动，副歌上行后稳稳落下，像一句“就这样吧”；注意旋律落回主音时和声也回到原位。再听**对比的幅度**。《Hallelujah! What a Savior》主歌压得很低，副歌突然打开，音高与音量一起上去，一分钟内完成从沉思到宣告。最后听**重复的确信**。《Jesus Loves Even Me》句子短、音域窄，副歌反复主句，孩子唱两遍就记住；重复在这里不是省事，而是把“确信”唱进身体。三首都是器乐改编，原版是独唱加簧风琴或钢琴。他的旋律很少用装饰音，句尾常留一个停顿，让会众接上下一句。

## 代表作

- 歌曲：《It Is Well with My Soul》《Hallelujah! What a Savior》《Jesus Loves Even Me》
- 其他：《Let the Lower Lights Be Burning》《Wonderful Words of Life》《Almost Persuaded》
- 编集：《Gospel Songs》等歌本

## 影响

布利斯的歌在他去世后继续被唱，尤其《It Is Well with My Soul》成为英语世界最常唱的安慰诗歌之一，也被爵士与流行歌手改编。**他把布道会的音乐写成一种可复制的情感结构**：疑问、上行、落下、重复。后来的福音诗歌与当代敬拜歌曲，仍在用这套结构处理悲伤与安慰。他去世时留下的歌本仍在继续印行，一印再印，在布道会现场被反复使用。

## Who he was

**Philip Paul Bliss** (1838–1876) was born in Rome, Pennsylvania, worked as a teacher, moved to Chicago in 1864, and moved from publishing and singing schools into work as a singing evangelist. He wrote words and music and compiled songbooks including Gospel Songs; Let the Lower Lights Be Burning, Wonderful Words of Life and Hallelujah! What a Savior spread within a few years. In December 1876 he and his wife were killed in the Ashtabula train disaster; he was thirty-eight.

## His place

Bliss worked as gospel song moved from revival meeting into homes and Sunday schools. His songs have two marks: **a clear division between solo verse and refrain**, the verse narrating and the refrain inviting, and **simple harmony with decisive turns**, often one ascent or one modulation that lifts the emotion into place. He shared a market and a set of songbooks with Sankey and Moody's circle; the music is church music, not concert repertoire.

## What to hear

Hear first **how the refrain answers**. In It Is Well with My Soul the verse has doubt and disturbance; the refrain climbs and then settles firmly, like a spoken "so be it". Notice that the harmony returns home as the melody reaches the tonic. Then hear **the size of the contrast**. Hallelujah! What a Savior keeps the verse low, then the refrain opens suddenly, pitch and volume rising together, moving from thought to declaration within a minute. Finally hear **the certainty of repetition**. Jesus Loves Even Me uses short phrases and a narrow range, the refrain repeating the opening; a child remembers it after two hearings. Repetition here is not laziness but a way of singing certainty into the body. All three are instrumental arrangements; the originals are solo voice with reed organ or piano.

## Key works

- Songs: It Is Well with My Soul, Hallelujah! What a Savior, Jesus Loves Even Me
- Others: Let the Lower Lights Be Burning, Wonderful Words of Life, Almost Persuaded
- Collections: Gospel Songs and other songbooks

## Influence

Bliss's songs kept being sung after his death, and It Is Well with My Soul became one of the most used hymns of consolation in English, arranged by jazz and popular singers too. **He turned revival music into a repeatable emotional structure**: doubt, ascent, settling, repetition. Later gospel songs and worship music still use that structure for grief and comfort.
