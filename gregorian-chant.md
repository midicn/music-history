::: zh
格里高利圣咏是罗马礼仪的单声部歌唱，也是西方音乐有谱可查的起点。它不靠拍子，而靠拉丁文的句法与重音组织旋律。

## 起源

六至七世纪，罗马唱诗班整理礼仪歌曲；八至九世纪，加洛林王朝为统一礼仪把它推行到阿尔卑斯山以北。后世把编纂归于教皇格里高利一世，传说他受鸽子启示记谱，实为权威包装。纽姆谱九世纪出现，十一世纪才逐渐精确。

## 听辨特征

只有一条声线，没有固定节拍，句尾以停顿换气。旋律围绕调式终止音，音域不宽，级进多于跳进。歌词有音节式与花唱式，前者一字一音，后者一个音节拖出长串音型。跟着拉丁文重音走，就能感到呼吸。

## 代表作曲家与作品

圣咏大多匿名。《慈悲经》属弥撒常规，《又圣母经》《天后怜我》是圣母对经，《创作者圣神请降临》是赞美诗。宾根的希尔德加德写单声部作品，音域与跳进更自由。

## 谱系

上承犹太会堂与拜占庭吟唱，下接奥尔加农、孔杜克图斯与经文歌；众赞歌与英国礼拜音乐也取用其素材。

## 常见误解

圣咏并非都出自格里高利本人，多数作品匿名，成书数百年。它也不单调，花唱段落可以华丽。圣咏不等于无伴奏合唱，管风琴常用来托住音高。

::: en
Gregorian chant is the single-voiced song of the Roman liturgy and the earliest Western music preserved in notation. It is organized not by beat but by the grammar and stress of Latin.

## Origins

The repertory took shape over centuries. From the sixth to seventh centuries Roman singers gathered liturgical songs; in the eighth and ninth the Carolingians spread Roman chant north of the Alps to unify worship. Later ages credited Pope Gregory I, though the dove story is legend. That legend gave the repertory authority rather than authorship. Neumes appeared in the ninth century as signs for contour, and only in the eleventh began to fix pitch.

## Listening features

There is one line, no fixed beat, and phrases end with a breath. Melody circles the modal final, stays in a modest range, and moves more by step than leap. Syllabic passages give one note per syllable; melismas stretch one syllable into a long run. Do not count beats; follow Latin stress and punctuation, and the music breathes.

## Composers and works

Most chants are anonymous. The Kyrie belongs to the Mass ordinary; Alma Redemptoris Mater and Salve Regina are Marian antiphons; Veni Creator Spiritus is a hymn. Hildegard of Bingen wrote monophonic pieces with a wider range and bolder leaps.

## Lineage

It draws on Jewish and Byzantine singing and leads to organum, conductus and motet; chorales and English liturgical music borrowed its melodies. Latin chant survives in Catholic worship today.

## Misconceptions

Chant was not all written by Gregory; most pieces are anonymous and the corpus grew over centuries. It is not a monotone recitation, since melismas can be elaborate. Nor does it mean unaccompanied singing: the organ often held the pitch.
