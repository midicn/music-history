## 问题

为什么近代音乐一再说城市？乡村也有声音，但城市把陌生人、噪声和夜生活压缩到同一条街。音乐怎样把这种密度变成可听的形状？

## 历史脉络

18世纪公共音乐会与咖啡馆让音乐离开宫廷，19世纪大都市、铁路与报纸制造了“观众”。20世纪录音、广播与夜店把城市声音商品化；与此同时，移民街区把故乡旋律塞进都市节奏。音乐会把城市切成可听的区块，教堂、剧院、码头与舞厅各有自己的声音。

## 关键案例

《纽约人行道》1894年出版，用街头口号与地名唱曼哈顿；蒂尔策的《巴黎风》把巴黎做成沙龙钢琴的明信片；奥里克《再见，纽约》写旅人离城。三首分别展示城市作为地址、气氛与中转站。

## 听什么

听低音是否像脚步，听旋律是否在模仿叫卖或汽笛，听段落有没有“到站”的停顿。

## Question

Why does modern music keep returning to the city? The countryside has sound too, but the city packs strangers, noise, and nightlife into one street. How does music turn that density into a listenable shape?

## Historical background

Eighteenth-century public concerts and coffee houses pulled music out of court; nineteenth-century metropolises, railways, and newspapers created an audience. Recording, radio, and nightclubs turned urban sound into a commodity in the twentieth century, while migrant neighborhoods folded homeland melodies into city rhythms.

## Key cases

The Sidewalks of New York (1894) sets street cries and place names to a singable tune. Tilzer's Parisienne frames Paris as a salon postcard, and Auric's Adieu, New York! turns departure into a piano farewell. The three show the city as address, atmosphere, and transit point.

## What to listen for

Hear whether the bass walks like footsteps, whether the tune mimics a vendor's cry or a train whistle, and whether a phrase pauses as if at a station.
