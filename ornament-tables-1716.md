::: zh
一个颤音到底颤几下，落在拍上还是拍前，十七世纪的演奏者各说各话。库普兰把常用装饰画成符号，附上解释，装饰音第一次有了可以照抄的说明书。

## 时间与地点

1716年，巴黎。库普兰出版《大键琴演奏法》，书末列出装饰音符号表。更早的表格出现在1689年当格勒贝尔的《大键琴曲集》里，巴赫与匡茨后来也各写了一份，时间跨到十八世纪中叶。

## 发生了什么

表里列出颤音、波音、倚音、滑音与琶音，每种配一个符号、一段解释和一条谱例。符号写在音符上方，演奏者按表执行，颤音从上方音开始，波音快速一碰，倚音占掉主音的一部分时值。库普兰还注明哪些装饰必须加，哪些可以省略，把演奏者的自由裁量收进一个范围。这套写法在法国宫廷流行，随后被德国与意大利的理论书转抄，但每个国家保留了自己的符号与奏法。

## 改变了什么

装饰从师傅带徒弟的手艺，变成可以印在纸上、跨城市传授的知识。出版商因此能把法国键盘风格卖到国外，业余学习者也能照表练习。代价是统一只是表面的，同一个符号在巴黎、德累斯顿与那不勒斯可能指不同的奏法，今天的演奏者仍要先判断自己面对的是哪一国的表。装饰音符号也让乐谱变得更精确，作曲家能指定细部，而不只写主干音。

## 听这个转折

先听库普兰《大键琴曲集》里的曲子，符号表出自同一人之手，听到的装饰就是表的执行。再听拉摩《大键琴曲集》第一册，装饰更密，常常写进旋律本身，与库普兰的克制形成对照。最后听亨德尔《和谐的铁匠》变奏，主题每次回来都换一套装饰，即兴与写谱在同一位作曲家身上并存。三首都是钢琴录音，听装饰落在拍前还是拍上。

::: en
How many notes a trill should hold, and whether it starts on the beat or before it, was a matter of local opinion in the seventeenth century. Couperin drew the common ornaments as signs, added explanations, and gave ornament a printed instruction sheet.

## Time and place

Paris, 1716. Couperin published L'Art de toucher le clavecin with a table of ornaments at the end. An earlier table had appeared in d'Anglebert's harpsichord pieces of 1689, and Bach and Quantz later wrote their own, carrying the story into the middle of the eighteenth century.

## What happened

The table listed the trill, the mordent, the appoggiatura, the slide and the arpeggio, each with a sign, a description and a musical example. The sign stood above the note and the player followed the table, trills beginning on the upper note, mordents snapping quickly, appoggiaturas taking part of the main note's value. Couperin also marked which ornaments were required and which optional, drawing the player's freedom into a defined range. The notation spread from the French court, and German and Italian treatises copied it, though each country kept its own signs and ways of playing them.

## What changed

Ornament moved from a craft passed between master and pupil to knowledge that could be printed and taught in another city. Publishers could sell French keyboard style abroad, and amateurs could practise from the table. The unity was partly on the surface. The same sign might mean different things in Paris, Dresden and Naples, and a modern player must first decide which national table is in front of them. The signs also made scores more precise, letting composers specify detail rather than only the main notes.

## Listen for the turn

Begin with a piece from Couperin's harpsichord books, since the table came from the same hand and what you hear is the table being executed. Then Rameau's first harpsichord book, where ornaments are denser and often written into the melody itself, a contrast with Couperin's restraint. Finally Handel's Harmonious Blacksmith variations, each return of the theme dressed in a new set of ornaments, improvisation and notation coexisting in one composer. All three recordings are on piano, so listen for whether the ornaments land on the beat or before it.
