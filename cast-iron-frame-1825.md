::: zh
木框钢琴的琴弦不能拉得太紧，否则框架会被拉弯。一整块铸铁框出现之后，琴弦可以更粗、更紧，钢琴的音量第一次够得上大音乐厅。

## 时间与地点

1825年，波士顿。制琴师巴布科克为一整块铸铁框申请专利，先用在小方琴上。奇克林在1840年代把它用到三角琴，斯坦威在1859年把交叉挂弦与整块铁框结合。欧洲厂商跟进得晚一些，十九世纪后半叶才普遍采用。

## 发生了什么

钢琴的琴弦总张力在十九世纪不断上升，木框靠铁条加固也撑不住。铸铁框是一整块带肋的金属板，弦轴、挂弦钉与音板都固定在它上面，张力由铁框承担，不由木壳承担。这一改动让琴弦可以加粗加长，低音更厚，音量更大，音准也更稳定。交叉挂弦把低音弦斜着拉过长弦上方，缩小琴身尺寸，同时增加弦长。制造随之工业化，铸铁、翻砂与装配成为钢琴厂的核心工序。

## 改变了什么

钢琴从客厅乐器变成音乐厅乐器。作曲家在更大的场地写作，钢琴声部可以与小提琴、乐队抗衡，李斯特与后来的拉赫玛尼诺夫把这种音量写进作品。钢琴制造业变成重工业，产量与出口随铁路扩张。代价是音色趋于统一，十九世纪初那种轻而透明的钢琴声音退到古乐演奏里。今天音乐厅里的三角琴，基本结构仍是1850年代定下的。

## 听这个转折

先听肖邦的第一叙事曲，写作年代早，钢琴的张力小，声音靠色彩与 rubato，不靠音量。再听李斯特的《侏儒之舞》，快速的弱音与突然的重击需要钢琴在大厅里站住，铁框开始起作用。最后听拉赫玛尼诺夫的前奏曲，低音厚，共鸣长，是现代铁框钢琴的声音。三首连起来，能听见钢琴怎样从室内走向大厅。

::: en
The strings of a wooden-framed piano cannot be pulled too tight, or the frame bends. A one-piece cast-iron plate allowed thicker strings at higher tension, and for the first time the piano could fill a large hall.

## Time and place

Boston, 1825. The maker Alpheus Babcock patented a one-piece cast-iron frame, first used in square pianos. Chickering applied it to grands in the 1840s, and Steinway combined overstringing with a full plate in 1859. European makers followed later, adopting it widely in the second half of the century.

## What happened

Total string tension rose throughout the nineteenth century, and wooden frames braced with iron bars could not keep up. The cast-iron plate is a ribbed metal bed to which the pin block, hitch pins and soundboard are fixed, so the frame rather than the wooden case carries the load. Strings could then be thicker and longer, the bass deeper, the sound louder, the tuning more stable. Overstringing slanted the bass strings across the longer treble strings, shrinking the case while lengthening the scale. Manufacturing became industrial, with casting, foundry work and assembly at the centre of the piano factory.

## What changed

The piano moved from the parlour to the concert hall. Composers wrote for larger rooms, piano parts could stand against violins and orchestras, and Liszt and later Rachmaninoff wrote that volume into their music. Piano making became heavy industry, with output and exports growing alongside the railways. The price was a more uniform tone, as the light, transparent pianos of the early nineteenth century retreated into historical performance. The grand piano in a hall today still rests on a structure settled in the 1850s.

## Listen for the turn

Begin with Chopin's First Ballade, written when tension was lower, its sound carried by colour and rubato rather than power. Then Liszt's Gnomenreigen, where rapid soft passages and sudden accents must stand in a hall, the iron frame beginning to do its work. Finally a Rachmaninoff prelude, thick in the bass and long in resonance, the voice of the modern iron-framed instrument. In sequence, they trace the piano from room to hall.
