::: zh
石版印刷把乐谱从手工刻版里解放出来，家庭第一次能买到刚在剧院听到的曲子。

## 时间与地点

1800 至 1900 年，伦敦、莱比锡、巴黎、米兰与纽约。石版印刷 1796 年发明，十九世纪二十年代起大量用于乐谱，一块版可以反复印，改几个音就能出新版。莱比锡与伦敦的出版商把目录做成系列，米兰的出版商围绕歌剧运作，纽约的出版区在世纪末集中到一条街，楼下就是琴行与排练房。

## 发生了什么

出版商买下作品，或按销售付版税，然后印出声乐谱、钢琴缩编、舞曲改编与单页歌谱。一部歌剧走红，其中的咏叹调会同时出现好几种编配，供不同水平的家庭使用。出版商按系列编号，封面写明改编者与演奏提示，销售靠目录、琴行与口耳相传。乐谱在琴行、百货公司与邮购目录里卖，推销员在店里反复弹唱新歌，把旋律塞给顾客。出版商在封底列同系列曲目，引导顾客一次买几本。版权公约之前，盗印在同一座城市并行，正版与翻版争同一批顾客。国际版权公约让乐谱成为受保护的财产，出版商开始跨国追查翻印。

## 改变了什么

音乐成为有标价、有上架周期的商品。大多数人不是通过乐队，而是通过钢琴缩编第一次听到交响曲与歌剧，家庭演奏就是当时的播放器。畅销谱与推销员催生了“热门曲”的概念，后来单曲唱片的逻辑在这里已经成形。乐谱封面开始印上歌手的肖像与剧院的名字，广告与评论一起制造需求。出版商成为守门人，作曲家则开始组织起来争取分成。

## 听这个转折

先听《圣洁女神》的钢琴改编，咏叹调被压成两只手，装饰简化，长气息改成可弹的句子。再听《枫叶拉格》，同一段切分主题反复出现，正是为反复弹奏而写。最后听《洋基少年》，主歌加副歌，篇幅短，适合客厅里站着唱。转折前，这些音乐属于剧院与乐队；转折后，它们被缩进家庭谱架。注意改编者删掉了什么，通常先删的是前奏与过渡。

::: en
Lithography freed music from the engraver's plate, and for the first time a household could buy the tune it had just heard at the theatre.

## Time and place

London, Leipzig, Paris, Milan and New York, from 1800 to 1900. Lithography was invented in 1796 and used for music in quantity from the 1820s; a plate could be reprinted, and a few notes changed made a new edition. Leipzig and London publishers built catalogues in series, Milanese houses worked around opera, and New York's music trade gathered in one street, with shops and rehearsal rooms below.

## What happened

Publishers bought works outright or paid a royalty on sales, then issued vocal scores, piano reductions, dance arrangements and single sheet songs. When an opera succeeded, its arias appeared in several arrangements at once, suited to different levels of household skill. Houses numbered their series and printed the arranger and playing hints on the cover, selling through catalogues, shops and word of mouth. Before copyright treaties, pirated editions ran in the same city and competed for the same buyers. Scores were sold in music shops, department stores and mail-order catalogues, while demonstrators played and sang new songs in the shop until the tune stuck with a customer. International copyright treaties made scores protected property, and publishers began pursuing unauthorised reprints across borders.

## What changed

Music became a commodity with a price and a shelf life. Most people met a symphony or an opera first through a piano reduction rather than an orchestra, and domestic playing was the playback of the day. The best-selling sheet and the demonstrator produced the idea of a hit, and the logic of the single record was already visible. Covers began to carry a singer's portrait and a theatre's name, with advertising and reviews manufacturing demand together. Publishers became gatekeepers, and composers began to organise for a share.

## Listen for the turn

Begin with the piano transcription of Casta Diva, the aria pressed into two hands, its ornament simplified and its long breath cut into playable phrases. Then Maple Leaf Rag, the same syncopated strain returning, written to be played again and again. Finally Yankee Doodle Boy, verse and chorus, short enough to sing standing in a parlour. Before the turn this music belonged to theatre and band; afterwards it was reduced onto a domestic music stand. Notice what the arranger deletes, usually the introduction and the transitions first.
