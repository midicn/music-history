::: zh
音符画出长短之后，乐曲还需要一个基准速度。中世纪的理论家用比例记号与 tactus 把这件事写成可计算的东西，节拍第一次有了纸面上的依据。

## 时间与地点

十三世纪到十六世纪，巴黎、尼德兰与意大利。十三世纪后期科隆的弗朗科整理有量记谱，十四世纪二十年代维特里的《新艺术》加入新的音值，十五、十六世纪的抄本与印本里出现各种比例记号。年代分散，无法归到一年。

## 发生了什么

有量记谱用音符形状表示相对长短，相对长短还需要基准。理论家把一拍称 tactus，通常相当于人的脉搏。拍子分三级，完整与不完整、大与小，用 O 与 C 加点的组合标记。比例记号写成 3、2 或分数，告诉歌手新的一段是上一段的两倍或一倍半快，三重比例常带三位一体的意味。歌手要同时读音符、读比例、读拍号，才能算出每个音的实际长度，一段音乐的速度因此是算出来的，不是商量出来的。

## 改变了什么

速度第一次成为可以写下来的参数，而不只是表演习惯。作曲家能在同一部弥撒里让不同段落成比例地加快或放慢，结构感因此更强。代价是记谱变得复杂，现代编辑要把这些比例翻译成 4/4、6/8 与节拍机数字，翻译的选择直接决定听起来的速度。小节线的观念也从这里长出来，节拍从手上的动作变成纸上的格子。

## 听这个转折

先听马肖《圣母弥撒》，各段的音值比例不同，听同一类旋律换比例后步伐怎样改变。再听奥克海姆《Fors seulement》，三个声部靠 tactus 对齐，慢速时每个音都沉下去。最后听若斯坎《武装的人弥撒》，段落的拍值在二与三之间切换，突然变宽或变窄就是比例记号在起作用。现代演奏的速度是编辑与指挥的选择，不是原谱给出的唯一答案。

::: en
Once note shapes showed relative length, a piece still needed a base speed. Medieval theorists made that speed calculable with proportion signs and the tactus, and the beat acquired a basis on the page.

## Time and place

From the thirteenth to the sixteenth century, in Paris, the Low Countries and Italy. Franco of Cologne organised mensural notation in the late thirteenth century, Philippe de Vitry's Ars nova added new note values in the 1320s, and manuscripts and printed books of the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries carry a variety of proportion signs. The dates are scattered and cannot be reduced to one year.

## What happened

Mensural notation used note shapes for relative length, but relative length still needed a reference. Theorists called the beat the tactus, usually compared to a human pulse. Time was graded in two levels, perfect or imperfect and major or minor, marked by combinations of O and C with dots. Proportion signs, written as 3, 2 or as fractions, told singers that a new section moved twice or one and a half times as fast as the last; triple proportion often carried a Trinitarian meaning. A singer had to read note shapes, proportions and time signs at once to work out each note's real duration, so tempo was calculated rather than negotiated.

## What changed

Tempo became a parameter that could be written down instead of a habit of performance. A composer could make sections of one Mass speed up or slow down in exact ratios, which strengthened large-scale shape. The price was complicated notation, and modern editors must translate the ratios into 4/4, 6/8 and metronome numbers, a choice that directly fixes the speed you hear. The idea of the bar line grew from the same ground: the beat moved from the hand to a grid on paper.

## Listen for the turn

Begin with Machaut's Mass of Our Lady, where sections use different proportions and the pace of similar melodic writing shifts with them. Then Ockeghem's Fors seulement, three voices aligned by the tactus, each note sinking in the slow passages. Finally Josquin's Missa L'homme arme, whose sections switch between two and three, the sudden widening or narrowing that proportion signs produce. A modern tempo is the choice of an editor and a conductor, not the single answer the manuscript gives.
