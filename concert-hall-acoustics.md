## 问题

同一支乐队，在两种厅里会像两支乐队。观众听到的不只是乐器，还有墙、地板与空气把声音送回来的方式。

## 历史脉络

十八世纪的音乐厅多是长方形鞋盒，莱比锡布商大厦、维也纳金色大厅、阿姆斯特丹音乐厅都靠这种比例。一九〇〇年波士顿交响大厅请萨拜因用混响时间计算座位与吸声，厅堂设计第一次有了公式。一九六三年柏林爱乐大厅把观众席围着乐队升起，声学家克雷默参与其中。此后多用途大厅常遭批评，二〇〇三年洛杉矶迪士尼音乐厅与二〇一七年汉堡易北爱乐厅又回到为声音定制的思路。

## 关键转折

转折是声学从经验变成可计算，又在计算之后回到听感。混响太长会糊，太短会干；侧向反射与早期衰变更难量化，却决定一个厅是亲切还是疏远。

## 听什么

先听亨德尔的焰火音乐，那是室外大编制，没有厅堂可借；再听《帕西法尔》，拜罗伊特的下沉乐池把乐队藏起来，人声浮在上面；然后听贝多芬第五的合奏版，鞋盒厅把弦乐与定音鼓黏成一片；最后听《一八一二年》，炮声与钟声要的是广场。

## The question

The same orchestra can sound like two different orchestras in two different halls. Listeners hear not only instruments but the way walls, floor and air return the sound.

## Historical arc

Eighteenth-century halls were mostly rectangular shoe boxes, and Leipzig, Vienna and Amsterdam all relied on those proportions. In 1900 Boston's Symphony Hall asked Wallace Sabine to calculate seating and absorption from reverberation time, giving hall design its first formula. In 1963 Berlin's Philharmonie wrapped terraced seating around the orchestra, with Lothar Cremer advising on acoustics. Multi-purpose halls often disappointed afterwards, and Los Angeles in 2003 and Hamburg in 2017 returned to rooms built for sound.

## The turning point

Acoustics became calculable, then returned to listening. Too much reverberation blurs, too little dries; lateral reflections and early decay resist numbers yet decide whether a hall feels intimate or distant.

## What to listen for

Start with Handel's fireworks music, written for a large band outdoors with no hall to borrow. Then Parsifal, whose covered pit at Bayreuth hides the orchestra under the voices. Hear Beethoven's Fifth in ensemble, the shoe-box hall gluing strings and timpani together. Finally the 1812 Overture, which wants a square, bells and cannon.
