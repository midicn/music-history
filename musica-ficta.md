::: zh
谱面上没有升号，也没有降号，唱出来的却常常带着半音。中世纪的歌手靠一套不成文的规则补上这些音，理论家把这种看不见的改动叫假音乐。

## 年代与城市

十三世纪到十六世纪，巴黎、佛罗伦萨与罗马的教堂和宫廷。没有哪一年可以标为起点，理论家从十三世纪起讨论假音乐，实践一直延续到巴洛克前夕。具体年代学界仍在争论，只能给世纪。

## 当时发生了什么

复调在终止前需要一个半音上行，遇到三全音要避开，六声体系里 mi 与 fa 的位置也要临时调整。这些改动多数不写进谱，由歌手按规则现场决定。常见做法是把终止前的音升高半音，或在 B 与 F 上选择降号，避开 mi 对 fa 的三全音。规则不是一部统一法典，各地各派的习惯不同，同一段音乐在不同教堂可能唱出不同的半音。理论家用一句口诀提醒歌手，la 之上的音要唱成 fa，说的就是降 B 的选择。

## 后来变了什么

半音化在键盘乐器普及之前就已经进入音乐，只是藏在表演惯例里。歌手必须懂理论，否则无法判断哪里该升哪里该降，演唱与理论因此绑在一起。记谱法后来把越来越多的临时记号写进谱面，到十六世纪末，作曲家开始自己标注升降号，表演者的裁量权随之缩小。今天的编辑仍在为这些没写出的音争论，录一首文艺复兴作品，先要决定用哪一版校勘。

## 听这一刻

先听若斯坎的《Mille regretz》，终止前那一下半音的推力多半由歌手补上，谱面并不标出。再听奥克海姆的《Fors seulement》，同一旋律在几个声部之间传递，每次终止都要重新决定升降；这里听到的是钢琴改编，半音由演奏者做出。最后听帕莱斯特里纳的《Aestimatus sum》，半音出现得更克制，终止仍靠细微的升高来收紧。录音里的这些半音大多出自现代校勘，不是原谱的笔迹。

::: en
The page shows no sharps and no flats, yet the singing often carried semitones. Medieval singers supplied them from unwritten rules, and theorists called the invisible change feigned music.

## Year and city

From the thirteenth to the sixteenth century, in the churches and courts of Paris, Florence and Rome. No single year marks a beginning. Theorists discussed musica ficta from the thirteenth century, and the practice ran to the edge of the Baroque. The chronology is still argued, so only centuries can be given.

## What happened then

Polyphony needed a semitone rising into a cadence, had to avoid the tritone, and had to adjust the place of mi and fa within the hexachord. Most of these changes were never written down; singers decided them on the spot by rule. The usual moves were to raise the note before a cadence, or to choose a flat on B or F so that mi would not collide with fa. The rules were not one code. Customs differed by region and by choir, so the same music might be sung with different semitones in different churches. One mnemonic told singers that a note above la should be sung as fa, which meant choosing B flat.

## What changed after

Chromaticism entered music before keyboards were common, hidden inside performance habit. Singers had to know theory, or they could not decide what to raise and what to lower, and so singing and theory became entangled. Notation then absorbed more and more accidentals, and by the late sixteenth century composers began marking them, shrinking the performer's discretion. Editors still argue about the unwritten notes, and anyone recording Renaissance polyphony must first choose an edition.

## Listen for this moment

Begin with Josquin's Mille regretz, where the semitone pushing into each cadence was probably supplied by singers and is absent from the page. Then Ockeghem's Fors seulement, in which one melody passes between voices and every cadence demands a fresh decision; the recording here is a piano arrangement, so the player makes the semitones. Finally Palestrina's Aestimatus sum, more restrained in chromaticism, its cadences tightened by a small rise. Most of the semitones you hear come from modern editions, not from the original ink.
