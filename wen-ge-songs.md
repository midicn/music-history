## 时间与地点

文革歌曲指1966年至1976年间在中国大陆广泛传唱的政治歌曲，场合包括学校、工厂、军营、街头、广播与批斗会。广播、电影与学校是主要传播渠道，歌本常以油印流传。1966年《毛主席语录》再版后，为语录谱曲的语录歌迅速流行；1968年后知青上山下乡，又出现知青歌。1972年起《战地新歌》陆续编印，收罗各地新作。

## 发生了什么

语录歌把政治条文配上简单旋律，李劫夫是代表人物，作品短小、易学、便于齐唱。颂歌与忠字舞音乐把领袖形象仪式化，广播与电影反复播放。1970年代初政治歌曲的创作略有恢复，旋律也更讲究。知青歌则在插队生活中产生，《南京知青之歌》1968年流传，后被批判。民间还有未公开传唱的地下歌曲，旋律更抒情。

## 音乐特征

文革歌曲以进行曲与颂歌为主，节拍方正，音域不宽，高音区多，强调齐唱与喊唱。旋律多用五声或大调，句尾常停在高音或主音；配器以铜管、鼓、手风琴与民乐为主。手风琴与民乐小乐队在基层最常用，专业录音则加铜管与合唱。语录歌短句重复，颂歌用上行跳进与延长音，知青歌则接近小调，带思乡与忧伤。

## 听什么

先听《东方红》，陕北民歌的骨架被仪式化，文革时成为开场曲；再听《毛主席是咱救星人》，颂歌体的高强硬响；再听《宣传歌》，政治宣传题材的民歌化写法；最后听《国际歌》，文革集会常唱，却不是文革创作。四例都不是文革原声。

## 常见误解

常见误解是以为文革歌曲只有语录歌。语录歌只是1966年后的一支，颂歌、进行曲、知青歌与地下歌曲同时存在。也有人以为文革音乐全都高硬，其实知青歌与地下歌曲常是慢速、抒情的小调；更不要把这些歌都当成文章作品，许多旋律来自民歌与旧歌。

## Time and place

Cultural Revolution songs means the political songs widely sung in mainland China from 1966 to 1976, in schools, factories, barracks, streets, broadcasts and struggle meetings. After the Quotations from Chairman Mao was reprinted in 1966, songs setting quotations to music spread quickly. From 1968, educated youths sent to the countryside produced their own songs. From 1972, the collection Zhandi Xinge printed new works from many regions.

## What happened

Quotation songs set political passages to simple tunes. Li Jiefu was the best-known composer; the songs were short, easy to learn and suited to unison singing. Praise songs and the music of loyalty dances ritualised the leader's image, repeated on radio and in film. Educated-youth songs grew out of village life; the Nanjing Zhiqing Song circulated in 1968 and was later criticised. Unofficial songs also circulated, more lyrical in tone.

## Musical features

The repertory is mainly march and praise song, with square metre, narrow range, high tessitura and a stress on unison shouting. Melodies are mostly pentatonic or major, phrases ending high or on the tonic; brass, drums, accordion and Chinese instruments dominate. Quotation songs repeat short phrases, praise songs use rising leaps and sustained notes, while educated-youth songs approach minor-key folk song with homesickness and sorrow.

## What to listen for

Hear The East Is Red, a Shaanbei folk frame ritualised and used as an opening number during the period. Then hear Chairman Mao Is Our Saviour, in the high, hard praise-song manner. Then hear Propaganda Song, a folk-style political number. Finally hear The Internationale, sung at rallies but not a Cultural Revolution composition. None is a period original.

## Common misunderstandings

The usual mistake is to reduce the period to quotation songs. They were one strand after 1966, alongside praise songs, marches, educated-youth songs and underground songs. Others assume all of the music was loud and hard; youth and underground songs were often slow, lyrical folk-style pieces. Nor were the songs all new; many melodies came from folk song and older repertory.
